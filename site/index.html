About this work

As we continue to build a new vision for Kuba, we know there are a lot of questions and concerns being shared across different spaces. This FAQ and site are intended to gather those questions in one place and respond as openly and clearly as we can.

The HSCF Interim Next Generation Committee will continue to update this document in real time as more questions come up. Our goal is to stay accountable, responsive, and focused on building with intention and care.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are you still working with or representing the Founding Fathers?

▶

No. To be as clear as possible: we are not affiliated with the Founding Fathers. They have fully stepped down, dissolved their transition team, and handed leadership of this work to the Interim Next Generation Committee members. Since that transition, we have not involved them in decision-making, messaging, or planning.

The Founding Fathers are learning about this work at the same time as the rest of the community. While we respect the foundation they laid, we are not here to uphold or extend their original vision unchecked. We are focused on building something that reflects our generation’s needs and future.

Are you pushing the elders out?

▶

No. We are not pushing elders out. They are an important part of the community and we value their presence, experience, and guidance.

Our goal is to create a structure where elders can engage productively and in an advisory role while allowing the next generation to take primary leadership in shaping and executing the vision.

Since the interim committee is made up of unelected representatives from Atlanta, how will you ensure diverse representation?

▶

We understand the concern. This committee is temporary, created to begin the transition and propose a new structure. We cannot hold meaningful elections without first creating a shared framework. We’ve started connecting with people in other cities to bring them in as partners and will expand the interim committee as next generation leaders from other communities join. After Kuba 2026, we will hold open elections for a permanent leadership body.

What skills and experience do you bring to this process?

▶

We bring professional experience in strategic planning, nonprofit management, organizational development, event production, facilitation, communications, and conflict resolution. We’re applying proven project design and management approaches while honoring the community’s legacy.

The interim committee is led by Maher Addish (Lead, Event Operations) and Hana Kamal (Lead, Strategy and Governance), both of whom bring years of relevant experience.

Isn’t this divisive?

▶

We believe labeling new ideas as "divisive" has often stopped honest dialogue. Our aim is not to divide—it's to build a more inclusive, modern, and accountable model that can evolve with our needs. Collaboration is central to what we’re doing, and we ask for imagination, openness, and a shared willingness to build something stronger together.

Why not just wait for the next election and lobby from within?

▶

The current structure does not function as a true federation and lacks central authority for standards, redistribution of resources, and accountability. Cities often operate independently without meaningful coordination, which leads to inequity. We're stepping forward to build a model we believe will better serve next generation Hararis and the diaspora.

When will there be a public conversation?

▶

We are planning a town hall-style gathering on Sunday, October 19th to share our proposed vision, goals, and structure. This will be a space for open discussion, reflection, and input. Please stay tuned for more details.

Things can’t change overnight, right?

▶

Correct — we’re not expecting instant change. We are prioritizing a gradual transition with a hybrid model, a multi-year roadmap (2–3 years), and staged milestones. We value local organizers and aim to empower local efforts while building shared standards and coordination.

I still have more questions.

▶
